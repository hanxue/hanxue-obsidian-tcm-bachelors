Back to the year 2023, when Summer was ending and the cooling days of Fall was fast approaching. Taichung experienced a strong typhoon, with strong winds and torrential rain. I have always wanted to experienced the power of a typhoon. Taichung's public transportation was halted for two days, and on the 3rd day, I took a public bus to Wuqi, right beside the sea. 

It was merely the tail-end of the hurricane, yet my standing meditation posture, solid and unmovable like a mountain, was easily uprooted by the remaining slight wind. 

Deep within my hear, I sorely wished I could directly feel the effects of a typhoon someday. I am sure that experience will be a great boost to my martial arts and spiritual cultivation. 

Shanghai, Summer of 2026. There were 3 typhoons that affected the city, 2 light ones and a strong typhoon. It's a shame that I fell sick when the strong typhoon befell Shanghai, and I could only rest in the school hostel. 

## Typhoons
Why do I like typhoons so much? 

Because it is difficult to observe Yin and Yang changes in normal circumstances. Sure, we know that Yang represents the day, Yin represents the night. We can further split them into Yin within Yang, Yang within Yang, Yang within Yin and Yin within Yin, the quadruplet hours of a day. But these are merely academic knowledge. 

What if by observing nature around us, we can see how Yin and Yang is reflected within ourselves? To know, to *feel*, to have insight on what exactly is Yin, and what exactly is Yang. The Eight-Principles Syndrome Differentiation includes Yin and Yang diagnosis, and we know that Vacuousness and Cold is Yin, Heat and Excess is Yang. Yet with all this knowledge, can we tell a person is "Yin" or "Yang"? Yin and Yang is all encompassing, so how can we regulate Yin or regulate Yang?

Without true understanding of Yin and Yang is simply to dwell in nonsensical Chinese Medicine. 

## Typhoon Bringing Insight
That's why I love typhoons so much. They tear apart the normal order of the world we can see, and turn everything topsy-turvy. Pressing urgency, high velocity, mixing everything together, and bringing changes. 

Today's Chinese Medicine shares the same root as Daoism. They share the same philosophical root and metaphysics of Yin-Yang and Five Elements. From the Daoist point of view, a human is a small version of the entire universe - hence you may frequently come across the term "microcosmic" in Daoist literature. Trying to understand the Heart-Mind directly in the process of spiritual cultivation is often difficult, because the barriers towards insight and knowing exist within ourselves. What if we can observe and understand nature, and as a reflection of our Heart-Mind gain penetrating insight of our inner self?

Take for example, Wind. 

The Wind from typhoon rises from the East and declines in the West. How does it begin? Heaven represents Yang, and Earth represents Yin, the heat from the Sun heats up the ocean's Water, gradually forming Cloud and Rain. Just as described in Huangdi Neijing 《素問·陰陽應象大論》, quoting from Paul U. Unschuld's translation: 

	The fact is,
	the clear yang is heaven;
	the turbid yin is the earth.
	The qi of the earth ri­ses and turns into clouds;
	the qi of heaven descends and becomes rain.
	Rain originates from the qi of the earth;
	clouds originate from the qi of heaven.

Doesn't this passage describe Earth's water circulatory system well? 

Now with intense heat from the Sun, and the Earth's Coriolis effect, having a huge amount of Cloud and Rain mixing together and at a very high energy level, the typhoon forms. It's worth pondering and trying to gain insight in Yijing (I-Ching)'s description of changes, as that will help us understand how a typhoon forms. 

How does a typhoon get depleted? It crosses the ocean, traverse unto the land and the Wind slowly extinguishes. In Liver related ailments in Chinese Medicine, you may hear the term Extinguishing Wind, or 熄風。Isn't this concept exactly as described in Chinese Medicine classics: Nourishing the Earth to Extinguish the Wind?

Typhoon **is** the interchange between Yin and Yang. No matter which angle you look at, it is Yin-Yang changes. Let's consider if the typhoon is something solid, or formless and conceptual. It is both and neither. Imagine trying to explain Wind to a child, we ask the child to experience the brush of wind against their face. You can't see it, you can't touch it, yet you know it exists. Through time immemorial, humanity has harnessed the strength of the wind to dry clothes, sail boats, and generate electricity. Without having a clear "scientific" definition of what is wind. 

To master Chinese Medicine, one has to gain insight to these formless, unseeable things.
