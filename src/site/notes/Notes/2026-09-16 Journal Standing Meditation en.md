## Standing Meditation

Today's journal entry will not touch upon my daily life, but will be sharing my experience in standing meditation instead. A little bit of background to where I am here: around 30 years old is turning point in my life. I decided to be true to myself, to pursue martial arts and spiritual cultivation. I was accepted as a disciple into the Wujiquan family of martial arts (https://www.wujiquan.net) . Wujiquan emphasize a trinity of standing meditation, martial forms practice and practical sparring, with standing meditation at its core. Even though standing meditation is important, if a practitioner ignores martial forms and sparring, their progress will be slow. 

Right now I am studying TCM at Shanghai University of Traditional Chinese Medicine, and I am experiencing a terrible inner struggle: my own experience of martial arts practice and Daoist cultivation is in conflict with the school's requirements and examination standards. As a result, there is a sea of turmoil in my heart, and my health is severely impacted. I want to let standing meditation be my daily habit, to get back on my life goals, to be free and experience inner peace. 

Due to my incomplete tasks yesterday and the fact that I rested a lot this morning, I only have limited time for standing meditation during the noon hours, right before my afternoon class. As a result, anxiety arise, I could not maintain peace and the meditation session ended in 12 minutes. My senior disciple brother shared before, 0500-0700 is an ideal time for standing meditation. This is when Yin and Yang in nature is in balance, and is good to harmonize Yin and Yang in oneself. Another good time to practice is between 2300-0100 before sleeping. When a day's tasks has been accomplished and there is not much on the mind, one can more easily relax and focus on the practice. In addition, this timing is also good for accumulating Qi for someone with Yin Deficiency such as myself. 

The state of mind crucially affects the results from standing meditation. There is really no magic or secret for this standing meditation technique. It unblocks and get Qi and Blood flowing, and solidify dispersed Qi. Therefore, when my mind is disturbed, standing meditation will result in the accumulation of turbid Qi. On the other hand, practicing with a calm heart, with no yearning or agitation, will result in the accumulation of clear Qi. Another way to see it is that standing meditation magnifies what one has; if one is at peace, there is more beneficial effects, and if one is agitated, there might be harmful effects. 

## Standing Meditation Experiences
When I am in a good condition, I can feel the difference between inhalation and exhalation. When Kidney is not deficient, breathing in goes deep, so deep as though it is absorbed to the bottomless East Sea. The inhalation process can keep going on and on, so lengthy that I am a little afraid to ponder how long I can keep inhaling. When Lung is not deficient, exhalation goes very broad, as though what is expelled goes to every corner of the world. 

What's strange is the fact that inhalation and exhalation sometimes do not balance each other out. Let's say I inhale for 30 seconds, and inhaled 1.5 liters of air. This can be followed by an exhalation of 2 minutes or more, and expelling more than the 1.5 liters that I inhaled. Of course it might be possible that I was sedentary for many hours, and my lungs accumulated a lot of air, and the lengthy exhalation process is simply removing said air. The strange thing is the short inhalation and long exhalation is repeated many times over. Where is this excesss air coming from? My understanding is that during standing meditation and in this scenario, my body is expelling unneeded turbid Qi. 

On the flip side, when my Kidney is not deficient, I can exhale for a very long time. When practicing with a calm mind, I have experienced very long inhalation. This is the absorption and solidifying of Clear Qi. Let's leave aside my school's deeper stages of standing meditation, this experience shows the human body has the innate ability to absorb and solidify clear Qi. 

Standing meditation also allow me to experience the different areas of my body that is unhealthy. In my current unhealthy state, I can clearly feel Heart functions to be worse off, especially in the upper left section of the chest, and occasionally in the upper right corner of the chest as well as Danzhong (middle of the chest). I can adjust the Danzhong by lightly using fingers to press at it, and my body posture will change to the "Contain the chest and raise the back" posture, common in Taijiquan and other internal martial arts. 

I can also feel my Life Gate (命門) to be damaged. Listening to the TCM theory being explained, I remember that the Life-gate concept is said to be something abstract, and possibly an earlier misunderstanding of kidneys, or else it is said to be the right kidney. This is a mistaken view, based on my own experience, the Life-gate is not the right kidney, and I can confirm that my injured Life-gate is directly related to the exhaustion or injury to the primordial Qi. 

Another problem that I identified with my posture is "floating". I feel like the upper part of my body is disjointed from the lower part, that the Conception Vessel and Governing Vessel's flow is blocked. There is disharmony of Water and Fire, I cannot get the Monarch Fire (Heart) to sink down to the Sea of Qi, and the Minister Fire to rise up to the middle of the chest. The waist and the hips are separated, and the body is no longer one whole system. The ileum (hip bones) are not pointed forward, thus causing both thighs and legs to be straightened an in tension, whilst the upper body is imbalanced, with the vertebrae not in a straight line. Another related problem is my raised shoulders; although not obvious when looking at my body, I can feel both arms disconnected from the rest of the body. During standing meditation there is lack of Qi flow into the arms, and there is a clear feeling of obstruction at the Danzhong. 

When my body is filled with turbid Qi, during standing meditation it becomes obvious that inhalation is much shorter than exhalation. When the situation is quite bad, I will often expel air and phlegm, and in really bad situation I may even experience a vomiting spell. After standing meditation, my body will dispel a lot of water via urination and sweating. Another curious experience is that prior to standing meditation I may drink 1 liter of water, but after standing meditation, I will easily expel 3-4 liters of water. Where is all this water coming from? It is Dampness. 


## Life-Gate
I won't be analyzing Life-Gate in depth, and this information is from the Internet. 

There is a saying that the concept of Life-Gate originates from basic understanding of human anatomy way back in history. But in Huangdi Neijing it says, 「太陽根至陰，結於命門，命門者目也。」Taiyang's root is Yin, they combine into Life-Gate, and Life-Gate is "seeing". 

With the modern understanding of the concept of sight, the eye and all the associated physiological aspects such as opthalmic and trigeminal nerve, optic chiasm, primary visual cortex, inferior temporal lobe and so on, we might mistaken the text as saying that Life-Gate equals to seeing. 

However, a deeper understanding of the use of ancient Chinese shows that Chinese people at that time do not separate abstract concepts and the physical world as we now do. In fact, the word 目 mu encapsulates not just the eye organ, the resulting nervous system pathway and the visual processing ability in the brain, but it also includes the processing, the feeling and the judging of the seeing. Perhaps a simple explanation is a scene from Avatar, where a Navii person points to his heart and say, "I see you". 

In Nan Jing (Scripture of Difficulties?), we find this passage 「腎兩者，非皆腎也，其左者為腎，右者為命門。命門者，諸神精之所舍，原氣之所系也。」Both Kidneys, they are not Kidney. The left one is Kidney, and the right one is Life-Gate. Life-Gate is where all Shen (Spirit) and Jing (Essence) resides, and is the system of Primordial Qi. 

Simply put, the left Kidney here is the Kidney-zang that we learn in Chinese Medicine theory, and the right Kidney is the Life-Gate, and is the Primordial Qi. Using modern Chinese interpretation of the sentence above, with the preconception of separating the abstract from the physical, will result in dire misunderstanding of the original intent of the author. 

The TCM theory where Kidney governs water, opens up to the ears, absorbs Qi, governs bones, governs the Brain-fluids, stores Essence, this is the left Kidney. Whereas the Primordial Qi, or you can think of it as the genetics or the pre-programmed information from our parents, and the concept of Minitrial Fire, is the Life-Gate or the right Kidney. Today's understanding of the hypothalamus-pituitary gland-adrenal gland system, someone's innate intelligence, predisposition or "destiny" of a person is closely related to the Life-Gate. The central nervous system and digestive system that forms a thinking axis, Kahneman's System II thinking, is related to Life-Gate.
